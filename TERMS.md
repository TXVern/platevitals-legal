## 2. Nutrition estimates — not lab results

Calorie, macro, and micronutrient numbers in PlateVitals come from AI photo analysis, barcode product lookup, typed food search, and similar tools. These numbers are **rough estimates for reference only**. They are **not** medical facts, laboratory results, prescriptions, or guaranteed measurements.

Estimates can be wrong or incomplete. Portion size, lighting, packaging data, AI mistakes, and product databases can all affect accuracy. Check labels yourself for allergens and anything that matters for your health. Use the numbers only as a personal tracking aid — never as medical or clinical information.

## 3. AI can be wrong

AI plate photo scan and typed AI food search can misidentify food, miss items on the plate, invent details, or give numbers that do not match the real meal. Treat every AI result as a rough draft you must review before you log it or act on it. AI output is **not** medical advice and **not** nutrition advice from a licensed professional.

## 4. Barcode and food database disclaimer

Barcode matches and product listings (including Open Food Facts and any local fallback list) can be wrong, outdated, or incomplete. Serving sizes and nutrition on packaging can differ from what the database shows. Always check the physical label yourself for allergens, ingredients, and anything that matters for your health. Wrong database data can lead to wrong totals in your log.

## 5. Health Connect and glucose — awareness only

Values shown from Health Connect (including continuous glucose monitor readings such as Abbott Lingo) may be delayed, incomplete, or missing. They are for **personal wellness awareness only** — not for emergency decisions, dosing, or treatment. Do not use PlateVitals instead of your CGM app, clinician, or emergency care.
